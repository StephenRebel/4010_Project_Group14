\section{Introduction}

This project aims to learn a policy from scratch which can generate musical compositions within the constraints of the Gymnasium (Maybe citations) environment we defined.
We framed the generation as a constrained Markov Decision Process (MDP). The core challenge faced was training purely with Reinforcement Learning (RL) in the absense of any supervised pretraining.
As such the performance of an agent is heavily dependant on the design and implementation of a strong reward function that provides representative and guiding feedback.

Understanding how an RL agent can learn musicality from interaction with an environment and receiving rewards provides insights into a lesser studied method of artificial music composition.
Additionally it allows the comparisson of this technique against more well established and traditional mthods such as supervised and unsupervised learning in deep neural networks.
Exploring the capabilities of RL agents in composing meaningful musical sequences is also transferable to studying the abilities of RL methods for long-term dependency and self-referential problems in real world planning.

Our state space is a musical composition of user defined number of bars (4 to 16 is often a reasonable range) defined as an array of bars. The bars vary in length as the agent generates notes and the internal representation is transformed into a flatten array for observation.
The action space is the constrained set of notes ranging over 2 octaves of the C major scale from MIDI pitch 60 to 84 in addition to a note lengths of either a sixteenth, eighth, quarter, half, or whole note and rests of corresponding length. There are a total 90 actions.
Our transition function is a deterministic in that the chosen note is appened (possibly after truncation to the the bar and applying a punishment), and updates the current beat before adding an additional bar to the internal musical score as appropriate. The terminal state is implicitly defined as a full score. Additionally the dynamics are implicit to the model as it only recieves observations and reward in reponse to its actions.
We provide a sparse reward to our agent (with the exceptions of penalties for invalid notes), computing a mathematical score for the completed musical score. This reward returned upon completion of a musical composition is a combination of rhythmic, harmonic, progression, and repetition properties as will be further dicussed later.
Due to the nature of musical composition we do not include any discount factor in our MDP formulation.
